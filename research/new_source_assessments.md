## NS1 — Zeng & Kim (2024/2025): "Chinese Firms in the US–China Trade War: Decoupling through Reshoring?"

- **Reference (APA 7):** Zeng, K., & Kim, S. Y. (2025). Chinese firms in the US–China trade war: Decoupling through reshoring? *International Studies Quarterly, 69*(1), sqae117. https://doi.org/10.1093/isq/sqae117 (article dated 2024 on the copyright line, issue year 2025 — confirm year form at reference-list assembly if approved)
- **Access:** Full text available (open access, CC-BY; PDF read in full — 17 pages)
- **Peer review:** Yes — International Studies Quarterly (Oxford University Press, International Studies Association); two anonymous reviewers acknowledged
- **Method:** Quantitative longitudinal analysis of firm-level Chinese *outbound greenfield investment* data
- **Geographical scope:** China (investor side); world regions (destination side)
- **Genuine reshoring status:** **No.** Despite the title, "reshoring" here denotes Chinese investors redirecting investment toward China's *domestic market* amid decoupling — i.e., reduced outbound FDI by Chinese firms. This is not the return of previously offshored manufacturing from an Asian host to a firm's home country in the thesis's sense; no Western-firm (or any firm's) Asia-to-home production return is studied.
- **Implementation relevance:** None (investment-flow patterns, not implementation processes)
- **Asia-specificity:** High as macro context (China FDI, trade war), zero as reshoring evidence
- **Evidence type:** Macro/firm-level FDI-flow evidence — contextual at best
- **New contribution:** Trade-war/decoupling investment patterns — territory already covered contextually by McCully & Simola (C05) and Xing et al. (C02)
- **Double-counting risk:** Overlaps the existing fragmentation/decoupling context sources
- **RECOMMENDATION: REJECT.** Fails criterion 7 (genuine reshoring) outright, and its title vocabulary creates active confusion risk — citing it in a reshoring thesis would invite exactly the scope error (presenting non-reshoring as reshoring) the protocol treats as a Critical issue. Its contextual value is already supplied by C02/C05.

## NS5 — Barbieri, Boffelli, Elia, Fratocchi, Kalchschmidt & Samson (2020): "What can we learn about reshoring after Covid-19?"

- **Reference (APA 7):** Barbieri, P., Boffelli, A., Elia, S., Fratocchi, L., Kalchschmidt, M., & Samson, D. (2020). What can we learn about reshoring after Covid-19? *Operations Management Research, 13*(3–4), 131–136. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12063-020-00160-1 (issue number to confirm at assembly; pages 131–136 verified from the PDF)
- **Access:** Full text available (PDF read in full — 6 pages)
- **Peer review:** Published in a peer-reviewed journal (Operations Management Research), but it is an **editorial** — an invited commentary, not a peer-reviewed empirical or systematic-review article
- **Method:** Editorial reflection; anecdotal short-term evidence from press releases (e.g., French and Italian firms returning production after Chinese supply disruptions); a short-term/long-term × firm/supply-chain framework for COVID-triggered reshoring decisions
- **Geographical scope:** Global; examples European, with China as disruption origin
- **Genuine reshoring status:** Discusses genuine reshoring, but its definition explicitly bundles near-shoring with back-reshoring — broader than the thesis's definition; would need careful handling
- **Implementation relevance:** Low — the content is decision/trigger-focused (COVID as trigger); implementation is mentioned only via citations to other work
- **Asia-specificity:** Contextual (China dependence, supply disruptions), no Asia-to-home implementation evidence
- **Evidence type:** Contextual/conceptual commentary; anecdotes are press-sourced, not researched cases
- **New contribution:** A citable, full-text-verified statement that COVID-19 acted as a *trigger/accelerator* of reshoring decisions — cleaner support for the Introduction's pandemic sentence than Somoza Medina (C04), whose index mixes relocation signals
- **Double-counting risk:** Moderate — same author team as A02 (Boffelli, Fratocchi, Kalchschmidt) and overlapping thematic territory with A12/C04; must never be counted as evidence alongside A02
- **RECOMMENDATION: APPROVE AS CONTEXTUAL SOURCE ONLY.** Suitable for the Introduction (and possibly Discussion) as context for the pandemic-trigger claim, with the editorial status named when relied on. Not eligible for the formal analytical sample (editorial; no primary or systematic evidence; decision-stage focus). If approved, assign ID C06, add to the corpus with role "Background/context — editorial", and permitted chapters Introduction; Discussion. Do not code into any barrier theme.
