## What could be established from available search snippets
Confidence: reasonably detailed and internally consistent across two independent search rounds (one general, one Altasia-focused), both converging on the same quantitative claims.

- The paper constructs a **Multi-Regional Input-Output (MRIO)-based global industrial value-chain/production-network model** (referred to in one search summary as GIVCBN-I/-II/-III null and counterfactual models) to estimate the potential **industrial-relocation risk exposure** of China's production network under substitution by other Asian economies.
- The central comparative concept is **"Altasia"** — a grouping described as encompassing roughly fourteen Asian economies including **Japan, South Korea, India, Bangladesh, and ASEAN member countries** — versus **ASEAN alone**.
- **Quantitative headline finding (converging across both search rounds):** the model estimates that considering **Altasia as a whole would induce roughly 25% of China's industrial and supply-chain linkages to "break"** (i.e., be substituted/relocated away), compared with **under 15% if only ASEAN is considered**. This indicates Altasia has a substantially stronger substitution effect on China than ASEAN alone, attributed to strong industrial complementarity among the Altasia economies.
- **Sectoral pattern:** the substitution effect is reported as concentrated in China's **resource-intensive and labor-intensive sectors**, while China is said to retain **substantial competitive advantages in capital-intensive and technology-intensive sectors** — i.e., the substitution/relocation risk is not uniform across all industries.
- **Policy-relevant claim:** reduced trade barriers within the Altasia grouping are argued to be capable of **accelerating** the relocation of China's industrial and supply chains, particularly in the resource- and labor-intensive sectors identified above.

## Explicit boundary check
This source does **not** describe genuine reshoring as defined for this thesis. It is a **macro, country/region-level input-output modeling study** of how a bloc of Asian economies (Altasia: Japan, South Korea, India, Bangladesh, ASEAN) could substitute for China within global production networks — i.e., a **China+1 / regional-diversification / intra-Asia substitution** phenomenon. There is no firm-level unit of analysis, no observed implementation process, and critically **no return of production to any firm's home country** — the entire phenomenon studied is relocation *between* Asian and Asia-adjacent economies, not a reshoring movement back to a Western (or any other) home base. This matches the corpus's own classification (L5, "outside core reshoring scope," "excluded from barrier coding") precisely. The source is usable only as **macro background** in the Introduction to characterize the broader Asian supply-chain-restructuring context (e.g., to explain why "China+1" and reshoring are conceptually distinct phenomena that this thesis must not conflate), and must never be coded as a driver or implementation barrier of reshoring, nor cited as firm-level evidence of any kind.
