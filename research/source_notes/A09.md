## Supplementary evidence from source_coding_notes.docx

Content below is drawn from a prior working conversation (resources/source_coding_notes.docx) that appears to have analyzed the source via NotebookLM against the actual PDF; treat as higher-confidence than the WebSearch-only evidence above, but still cite verification status per claim. A09 is flagged as a core thesis source, so this review is especially thorough.

**Design confirmed and extended:** 16 Swedish manufacturing firms, semi-structured interviews — matches the existing note. New detail: the docx describes a **"four-level coding"** procedure and confirms **65 drivers, 32 barriers, 38 enablers** (matching the existing note's abstract-derived figures). Verification status: DIRECT for the 65/32/38 figures (stated plainly, consistent across both notes); PARTIAL for "four-level coding" (stated once, no elaboration of what the four levels are).

**Host-country detail — fills a gap the existing note explicitly flagged as missing:** The docx states plainly that the article's sample/discussion **includes China and Poland** as offshore/host countries ("Статья включает Китай и Польшу, но отдельные findings могут относиться: к China cases; к Poland cases; ко всей Swedish sample; к literature review"). This is new and directly addresses the existing note's "Sample detail: ... prior offshore host countries of the 16 Swedish firms are not in the snippets" gap. Verification status: **PARTIAL, not DIRECT** — the docx states this as a determination from its own reading of the article, but does not reproduce a verbatim quote, table, or page reference naming China/Poland; the docx itself repeatedly cautions that **not every barrier is automatically China-specific** just because China appears somewhere in the article, and that a barrier should only be coded as China-specific when a quote, respondent description, case table, or explicit text ties it to China. This caution should be preserved in the thesis-facing note: China is confirmed to appear as a host country in this study, but individual barrier-to-China attribution still requires full-text verification.

**Correction to the existing note's treatment of "barriers" as a single undifferentiated set:** The docx explicitly warns that **not all 32 barriers are implementation barriers** — many are general decision-stage factors. It splits them into:
- *Direct implementation barriers:* strict host-country layoff regulations, patent/ownership complications, insufficient internal capacity, competence shortages, facilities/equipment constraints, actual transition-cost problems (as opposed to planning-stage cost misestimation).
- *General/decision-stage barriers* (may affect both decision and implementation but need contextual verification): high domestic labour costs, overhead costs, small home market, loss of logistics priority, general labour scarcity.

**Six primary implementation barriers with explicit codes (substantial new detail, not in the existing note):**
1. **HOST_COUNTRY_EXIT_REGULATION** (Regulatory/Institutional, secondary Cost/Operational; stage: foreign disintegration/exit; theory: TCE) — strict host-country layoff regulations, inability to quickly close/reduce offshore operations, locked investments, substantial exit costs. The docx calls this "one of the most valuable findings ... because it shows a real barrier to exiting an Asian host market." Verification status: **PARTIAL** — the barrier itself (exit regulation/layoff rules) is stated plainly, but the explicit "Asian host market" framing is the docx conversation's own interpretive gloss, not a reproduced quote tying it specifically to China; treat the barrier as confirmed-general and the Asia linkage as unverified until checked against the full text.
2. **SUPPLIER_IP_DEPENDENCE** (Asset/Capability + Contractual/Knowledge; stage: transfer/disengagement; theory: TCE+RBV) — firm may not fully control rights to a component/technology and therefore cannot simply move production home (asset specificity, contractual dependency, supplier opportunism, switching costs; control of strategic knowledge).
3. **DOMESTIC_CAPACITY_SHORTAGE** (Asset/Capability; stage: reintegration/ramp-up; theory: RBV) — lack of production facilities, equipment, routines, organisational capacity despite a reshoring decision having been made.
4. **INTERNAL_COMPETENCE_GAP** (Asset/Capability — HR; stage: reintegration/implementation; theory: RBV) — competencies lost over years of offshoring.
5. **FACILITIES_EQUIPMENT_CONSTRAINT** (Asset/Capability + Cost/Operational; stage: preparation/ramp-up; theory: RBV).
6. **RESHORING_COST_MISCALCULATION** (Cost/Operational; stage: decision+implementation; theory: TCE/bounded rationality) — the docx cautions this should be coded as a primary implementation barrier "only where the article shows actual cost difficulties during or after relocation, not simply a pre-estimation error."

All six: Verification status PARTIAL — barrier labels and category/theory mapping are stated plainly and specifically in the docx's coding tables, but no verbatim article quotation is reproduced for any of them, so DIRECT cannot be claimed per the stated verification rule.

**Correction — IT integration is NOT primary empirical evidence from the 16 interviews:** The docx explicitly flags that "IT integration" (mentioned in the existing note only implicitly as a "supporting barrier") is drawn from the article's **literature-review component**, not from the 16 empirical interviews. It should be coded as a "review-level supporting barrier," not as direct empirical evidence from A09's own fieldwork. **This is a genuine correction to how this barrier should be weighted** if it is cited from A09 — it must not be presented as a firm-level empirical finding from the Swedish interview sample.

**New temporal caveat (not in the existing note):** The docx states the interviews underlying this article were **conducted in 2018**, although the article was published in 2025. It notes this "does not invalidate the findings" but recommends the Discussion account for this time gap when interpreting currency of the evidence (e.g., relative to post-2020 supply-chain disruption and Industry 4.0 developments).

**Enablers detail relevant to Sub-Question 2 (managerial implications) — new, more specific than the existing note:** automation (compensates labour-cost disadvantage, worker shortage, productivity gap); labour productivity (assess output/productivity, not wage level alone); total-cost perspective (exit, transition, reintegration costs, equipment, facilities, workforce, IP dependencies); early capability assessment before implementation begins (skills availability, production capacity, IP ownership, equipment, supplier dependencies, IT compatibility).

**Theory-framework nuance:** The docx confirms OLI (Dunning's eclectic paradigm — resource-seeking, market-seeking, efficiency-seeking, strategic asset-seeking) is **explicitly used by the article itself**, matching the existing note. It clarifies, however, that **TCE and RBV mappings are the thesis's own interpretive overlay** applied to the findings (exit costs/layoff regulations/contractual constraints/patents/supplier dependence/cost miscalculation → TCE; skills/capacity/equipment/facilities/competencies/automation → RBV), not necessarily the article's own stated theoretical framework. This distinction should be preserved when writing Discussion — attribute OLI to the authors, and TCE/RBV interpretation to the thesis's own analytical framework.

**No outright contradiction of the existing note was found**, but two genuine refinements/corrections should be carried into any thesis-facing summary of A09: (1) IT integration is review-derived, not primary-empirical, within this source; (2) the 32 "barriers" are not uniformly implementation barriers — roughly six are primary implementation barriers, the rest are general/decision-stage factors requiring contextual caution before use in Results. The China/Poland host-country detail is a substantive addition (not a contradiction) that partially closes a gap the existing note had explicitly flagged as unresolved, but individual barrier-to-China attribution still requires full-text confirmation before being presented as Asia-specific in Results.
