Abstract-level information, with somewhat more methodological detail than typical for the other sources searched (the MDPI abstract appears to be reasonably well indexed).

- Context/motivation stated in the abstract: amid changes in markets and government regulations, organizations that previously offshored production began returning to their country of origin (reshoring); these relocation/business-model decisions now occur in the context of Industry 4.0, described as a technology paradigm with the potential to transform supply chains and influence organizational aspects through "innovative solutions and resource efficiency."
- Method: a systematic literature review that identified **43 articles** relating to reshoring and Industry 4.0, with an observed increase in publication volume in recent years.

## Supplementary evidence from source_coding_notes.docx

Content below is drawn from a prior working conversation (resources/source_coding_notes.docx) that appears to have analyzed the source via NotebookLM against the actual PDF; treat as higher-confidence than the WebSearch-only evidence above, but still cite verification status per claim.

**Sample size detail confirmed and extended:** 43 articles in the final review, screened down from an **initial pool of 1,048 documents** (the "1,048" figure is new — not in the WebSearch note). The docx also notes the search/screening process concluded in **February 2020**, meaning the review does not cover more recent (post-2020) reshoring/Industry 4.0 literature — a useful methodological caveat for Methodology or Discussion. Verification status: DIRECT for the 1,048→43 figures (stated plainly in the docx table); PARTIAL for the February 2020 cutoff (stated as a fact but without a reproduced quote).

**Barrier list with explicit codes (more granular than the WebSearch note's "9 aspects, 3 named"):**
- TECHNOLOGY_IMPLEMENTATION_READINESS — insufficient managerial/organisational readiness to adopt Industry 4.0 (Asset/Capability + Cultural/Organisational)
- TECHNOLOGICAL_CAPABILITY_GAP — lack of competencies/maturity for Industry 4.0 use (Asset/Capability)
- LACK_OF_VALID_BUSINESS_MODEL — absence of a business model/strategy preceding technology choice (Cultural/Organisational)
- COST_COORDINATION_COMPLEXITY — difficulty controlling/coordinating costs (Cost/Operational)
- IMPLEMENTATION_COST_ESCALATION — risk of rising costs when reorganising production/supply chain (Cost/Operational)

All coded as **review evidence**. Verification status: PARTIAL — these are the docx's own paraphrased labels, not verbatim quotations.

**Explicit caution on what NOT to code as implementation barriers (new, useful guardrail):** The docx warns that physical distance, cultural distance, "dispersion distance," and IP theft — although discussed in the reviewed literature — are more accurately drivers of reshoring (motivating firms to leave the offshore location) or decision/coordination problems specific to *offshoring*, not implementation barriers to the *return*. It explicitly states IP theft "is a typical driver... not an implementation barrier." This directly reinforces CLAUDE.md's own driver-vs-barrier distinction rule and should guide how any A07-derived claim is framed in Results.

**Geographic composition clarified (relevant to the corpus's "Medium" Asia specificity rating):** The docx states the 43/1,048-document review spans Europe, Asia, the Americas, and Oceania, and that **eight of the underlying studies are Asian**. However, it explicitly cautions that the presence of eight Asian studies does not mean they analyze a *return of production from Asia to a home country* — "Asia relevance is present, but must not be overestimated." This nuances (but does not contradict) the existing note's observation that no Asia-specific finding could be confirmed from search snippets; it explains *why* the corpus assigned "Medium" Asia specificity while confirming no disaggregated Asia-specific barrier finding exists.

**Theory-mapping caveat:** The docx notes that TCE and RBV links (coordination/control costs and uncertainty for TCE; digital capabilities and organisational maturity for RBV) are the *thesis's own interpretation* — "the article itself, according to the analysis, is not strictly built on TCE and RBV." This should be flagged if TCE/RBV are attributed to Espíndola et al. directly rather than presented as the thesis's interpretive lens.

**No factual contradiction found** with the existing WebSearch-based note; the docx fills in the screening numbers, names the remaining thematic content, and adds an important driver-vs-barrier guardrail the WebSearch note could not establish.
