## What could be established from available search snippets
Search results consistently describe this as a conceptual paper addressing how reshoring — and its effects on global trade — has drawn increasing attention from practitioners, policymakers, and scholars. The retrieved abstract-level text identifies specific challenges associated with reshoring: supply chain disruption, labour shortages, and sustainability issues. It frames Industry 4.0 technological advancements — specifically cloud-based artificial intelligence technologies and "efficient remote management" — as enablers that can help address these challenges. One snippet elaborates that Industry 4.0 allows firms to reassess sustainability issues through improved data processing (e.g., supply-chain information), which may encourage reshoring decisions, and that these technologies create opportunities for firms to reorganize supply chains/material flows and reduce reliance on cheap labour, which in turn affects "x-shoring" (a general term covering offshoring/reshoring/backshoring type) decisions. The paper is also described as tracing a shift in Industry 4.0 "from being technology-driven to value-driven," and it proposes recommendations for future research — consistent with a conceptual/review contribution rather than a primary empirical study.

Content below is drawn from a prior working conversation (resources/source_coding_notes.docx) that appears to have analyzed the source via NotebookLM against the actual PDF; treat as higher-confidence than the WebSearch-only evidence above, but still cite verification status per claim.

**Confirmed framing (upgrades WebSearch note):** The docx confirms this is a conceptual/agenda-setting article, not a primary empirical study — the authors conducted no interviews, surveys, or longitudinal case observation of any specific China-to-home implementation process. The "10 secondary marketplace cases" mentioned in the approved corpus rationale are confirmed by the docx to be based on **secondary materials** (company reports, press releases, media sources) — explicitly, they should **not** be treated as ten independent empirical cases in the thesis's SLR. This directly addresses the WebSearch note's largest flagged gap (identity/content of the marketplace cases) at the level of provenance (secondary/media-based), though specific case contents/countries are still not given in the docx excerpt reviewed.

**Central conceptual contribution — Industry 4.0's dual role (new detail not in WebSearch note):**
- As **enabler**: can reduce labour intensity, raise domestic productivity, improve material-flow control, support quality and traceability, enable flexible production, and offset high home-country costs.
- As **source of new requirements**: deploying the technology itself requires digital skills, investment capital, IT integration, data governance, cybersecurity, and production-planning capabilities, plus organisational adaptation — i.e., automation does not automatically remove barriers.
