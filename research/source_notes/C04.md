## Explicit boundary check

This source does **not** describe genuine reshoring in the thesis's strict sense (a specific, observed Asian-host-to-home-country return of manufacturing at firm level). It is a **European macro/regional context study** examining deindustrialization and a broader reindustrialization/reshoring trend across large European companies in the post-Great-Recession and COVID-19 period, using a constructed involvement index rather than firm-level implementation observation. Two boundary issues follow directly from the corpus rationale and from what search snippets could establish:

1. **Not Asia-specific evidence.** Nothing found ties the study's index or findings to a disaggregated Asian host country; any prior offshoring location is referenced only generically ("third countries" with lower labour costs). It cannot be used to support an Asia-specific claim.
2. **The index conflates relocation types.** Per the corpus's own inclusion/exclusion rationale — corroborated by the search finding that the index is a company-level "involvement" score rather than a direct count of completed reshoring moves — the scoring approach mixes reshoring together with domestic expansion and other relocation signals. This means any company classified as "reshoring-involved" by the index may not have executed an actual return of previously offshored production; the construct validity for the thesis's strict reshoring definition is weak.

No content found or coded from this source should be treated as implementation-barrier evidence: it is macro/company-index context on the scale and post-pandemic reinforcement of reshoring as a phenomenon in Europe, not evidence of what firms encountered while implementing a return of production.
