## Methodological procedure established from available search snippets

The article was published in an open-access journal (BMC Medical Research Methodology); a PMC (PubMed Central) copy exists (PMC2478656), and a City University open-access repository copy is also indexed, though direct fetch of publisher/repository domains was not attempted per the task's network constraint — the following is based on WebSearch summaries of these open-access copies, which is a comparatively reliable secondary route for this particular source since PMC abstracts/summaries closely mirror the primary text.

**Three-step thematic synthesis procedure**, consistent with the corpus classification's own description ("line-by-line coding, descriptive themes and analytical themes"):

1. **Line-by-line coding of the findings of primary studies.** The text of included studies (specifically their reported findings/results sections) is coded line by line, similarly to how a single qualitative primary study would be coded — codes capture the meaning and content of sentences/statements across the set of studies being synthesized.
2. **Development of descriptive themes.** Codes are then organized and grouped into descriptive themes. This step remains "close" to the primary studies — i.e., descriptive themes are essentially a structured, higher-order organization of what the primary studies already report, without adding interpretation beyond the source material.
3. **Generation of analytical themes.** From the descriptive themes, the reviewers generate analytical themes, which represent a further stage of interpretation. At this stage the reviewers "go beyond" the content of the primary studies to produce new interpretive constructs, explanations, or hypotheses that were not explicitly stated in any single included study but emerge from synthesizing across the set.

This descriptive-then-analytical-themes distinction is the core contribution most consistently cited when Thomas & Harden (2008) is referenced in later methodology literature, and is corroborated by multiple independent search summaries in this search round (converging on identical language: "line-by-line coding," "descriptive themes... close to the primary studies," "analytical themes... go beyond the primary studies").

**Stated rationale (per search summaries):** thematic synthesis is presented by the authors as a method that preserves an explicit and transparent, auditable link between the review's conclusions and the original text of the primary studies — i.e., a reviewer or reader can trace an analytical theme back through the descriptive themes to the specific coded text passages in the primary studies. This transparency/traceability rationale is offered as a strength relative to less structured qualitative synthesis approaches, positioning thematic synthesis as compatible with the rigor expectations of systematic reviewing while still permitting the kind of interpretive theory-building associated with qualitative research synthesis.
