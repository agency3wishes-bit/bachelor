## What could be established from available search snippets
Confidence: abstract-level, corroborated across multiple independent sources (Springer Link summary, Semantic Scholar, ResearchGate, and a related student-research derivative article that cites the same underlying phenomenon).

- The paper is a **preliminary/exploratory investigation** into the relocation of labour-intensive manufacturing firms **from coastal China (specifically the Pearl River Delta, PRD) to Southeast Asian countries**, identified in search summaries as **Indonesia, Vietnam, and Cambodia**.
- The stated motivating context is that, since approximately 2000, export-led coastal Chinese regions faced rising labour costs, prompting spatial relocation of manufacturing to lower-cost locations — both **inland China** (intra-China relocation) and **neighbouring Southeast Asian countries** (cross-border relocation).
- The industries examined are explicitly **garment and electronics manufacturing**, described as labour-intensive, environmentally polluting, low-value-added sectors that the Chinese government had targeted for industrial restructuring/upgrading since around 2005.
- The paper positions itself as filling a research gap: prior literature had examined coastal-to-inland-China relocation extensively, but comparatively little had examined **cross-border relocation out of China into Southeast Asia** — i.e., the paper's own framing confirms its subject is intra-Asian relocation, not a return to any firm's home country outside Asia.

## Explicit boundary check
This source does **not** describe genuine reshoring as defined for this thesis (the return of previously offshored manufacturing from an Asian host country to the firm's home country). It describes the opposite direction and a different phenomenon entirely: labour-intensive manufacturing relocating **out of coastal China and into other parts of Asia** (inland China and Southeast Asian countries — Indonesia, Vietnam, Cambodia). This is a textbook example of **intra-Asia relocation / China+1-type diversification / re-offshoring**, explicitly named as such in the corpus rationale ("re-offshoring/China+1, not reshoring"). No home-country return to a non-Asian location is described anywhere in the available search material. The source is therefore usable strictly as **background/context** on the dynamics of Chinese and Southeast Asian manufacturing cost structures — it must never be cited as evidence of reshoring, and it must never be coded into the barrier framework as either a driver or an implementation barrier of reshoring.
