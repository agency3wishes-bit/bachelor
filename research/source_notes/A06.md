## What could be established from available search snippets
Confidence: abstract-level solid; specific barrier wording partial (some snippet content blended with other reshoring papers in the same result sets).

**Method:** Systematic literature review on "reshoring," primarily qualitative technique. A structured keyword search followed by elimination yielded **22 peer-reviewed journal papers** in the final review. This 22-paper figure was returned consistently and is reliable at abstract level.

**Purpose:** To clarify the "blurry" concept of reshoring and its main drivers and barriers, and to give an overview of the scientific theories used in prior reshoring research.

**Central organising result (reliable):** Drivers and barriers are grouped into **five sets of dynamics: (1) global competitive dynamics, (2) home country, (3) host country, (4) supply chain, and (5) firm-specific.** This five-category grouping was returned consistently across multiple queries and is the paper's signature structure. Note this is Wiesmann's *own* taxonomy and is NOT identical to the thesis's four-category framework (Regulatory/Institutional, Cultural/Organisational, Cost/Operational, Asset/Capability); a mapping step is required.

**Additional finding:** "There is currently no consensus on the definition or 'theory of reshoring'" — supports the thesis's conceptual-clarification and definitional sections.

**Barrier content (partial confidence):** One barrier-focused query returned statements that reshoring firms can fail because "they lose access to vital resources for production that were available in the offshore location but that do not exist in the home country," and that "access to skilled personnel is a problem in both the host country and home country," plus supply-chain barriers around "home country suppliers' capacity building and local networks and supply chain integration." These are consistent with the corpus rationale (capacity, competences, labour, supplier dependence, infrastructure) but the snippet co-mingled results from other papers, so exact wording/attribution to Wiesmann is not certain.

## Barrier-relevant claims (coded)

Claim 1
- Claim: Reshoring drivers and barriers can be organised into five sets of dynamics — global competitive, home-country, host-country, supply-chain, and firm-specific.
- Driver vs. implementation barrier: Covers both drivers and barriers (the paper explicitly separates them but shares the five-dynamics scaffold).
- Firm-level vs. macro-level: Mixed by design — "firm-specific" is firm-level; "global competitive" and "host/home country" are macro/contextual.
- Asia-specific: No (general review; corpus Asia specificity Medium reflects host-country coverage, not disaggregated Asian findings).
- Suggested framework category: Does not map cleanly — it is a parallel taxonomy that must be *cross-walked* to the thesis's four categories (e.g., firm-specific → Asset/Capability + Cultural/Organisational; home/host country → Regulatory/Institutional; supply chain → Cost/Operational).
- Verification status: DIRECT (five-dynamics grouping confirmed repeatedly).

Claim 2
- Claim: A barrier to reshoring is loss of access to production resources available in the offshore location but absent in the home country.
- Driver vs. implementation barrier: Implementation barrier (loss of resource base impedes the actual return).
- Firm-level vs. macro-level: Firm-level (resource access) with home-country infrastructure dimension.
- Asia-specific: No — stated generically about "the offshore location," not specifically Asia.
- Suggested framework category: Asset/Capability (loss of resources/competences), with a secondary Cost/Operational link.
- Verification status: PARTIAL (snippet supports it, but wording blended with other sources; confirm against Wiesmann's own text).

Claim 3
- Claim: Access to skilled personnel/competences is a barrier in both host and home country; a lack of firm-level competences can undermine reshoring success.
- Driver vs. implementation barrier: Implementation barrier (skills/competence shortage obstructs rebuilding production).
- Firm-level vs. macro-level: Firm-level (competences) plus labour-market (macro) dimension.
- Asia-specific: No.
- Suggested framework category: Asset/Capability (skills/competences).
- Verification status: PARTIAL.

Claim 4
- Claim: Supply-chain barriers include weak home-country supplier capacity, local networks, and supply-chain integration (supplier dependence built up during offshoring).
- Driver vs. implementation barrier: Implementation barrier (re-establishing a domestic supply base is a difficulty of the return).
- Firm-level vs. macro-level: Mixed (firm supplier relationships + regional supplier ecosystem).
- Asia-specific: No.
- Suggested framework category: Cost/Operational (supply-chain reconstruction) with Asset/Capability overlap (supplier dependence as a lost capability).
- Verification status: PARTIAL.

Claim 5
- Claim: Decision-process problems (the difficulty and immaturity of reshoring decision-making) are among the barriers reviewed.
- Driver vs. implementation barrier: Pre-implementation / decision barrier — borderline; per protocol, decision-process difficulty is not itself an implementation barrier unless it obstructs the return.
- Firm-level vs. macro-level: Firm-level.
- Asia-specific: No.
- Suggested framework category: Cultural/Organisational (managerial/decision capability) — tentative.
- Verification status: UNVERIFIED — asserted in the corpus rationale ("decision-process problems") but not independently confirmed by a snippet; treat as inferred until checked.
