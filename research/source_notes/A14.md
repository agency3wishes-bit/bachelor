## What could be established from available search snippets
High confidence on study design and headline finding; the corpus characterization is well corroborated.

The abstract (retrieved via WebSearch summary drawing on the MDPI/PMC listing) states the study uses an **extended fuzzy DEMATEL (Decision-Making Trial and Evaluation Laboratory)** method to analyze interrelationships among reshoring decision criteria and their sequential effects, using **data from the UK apparel industry as a baseline**. The stated motivation is that "myopic financial factors that once made overseas locations attractive for offshoring" are being revised, and COVID-19 "highlighted the need for restoring the previously offshored competencies." The method is explicitly framed as a **decision-support/decision-analysis tool** (expert-based, DEMATEL), not an implementation-process case study.

Headline finding: **supply process complexity** has the highest potential impact on the financial criterion among the decision criteria studied; **environmental sustainability** also strongly influences cost-efficiency, which the abstract frames as the major driver of the original offshoring decisions.

One search summary sentence asserted that "the study examined apparel reshoring decisions **to Asia and China** by analyzing how various decision criteria interrelate..." — **this specific claim (explicit China/Asia destination framing) is flagged as UNVERIFIED.** It appeared only in the WebSearch tool's own generated summary text, not inside a quoted abstract passage, and it is not consistent with the directly quoted abstract text (which describes the UK apparel industry as the data baseline for a general reshoring-decision-criteria study, without naming Asia or China as the offshoring origin). This should not be relied upon or cited; it may be a search-summary overreach rather than a genuine finding of the paper. Apparel-industry offshoring commonly involves Asian manufacturing origins as background context, but the paper's own Asia-specificity, per the corpus, is only "Low–Medium," which is consistent with treating this as background/plausible rather than confirmed.

**Sample detail — more specific than the existing note.** The docx specifies the expert panel as **three experts from British apparel SMEs** (not just "UK apparel industry as a baseline"). Method confirmed as extended fuzzy DEMATEL analyzing **13 decision criteria** and their causal interrelationships.

**Asia/China destination claim — CONFIRMS the existing note's caution; the docx makes the same call independently.** The docx explicitly states that the specific host country of the three studied firms is **not identified** in the paper: China, Vietnam, Thailand and other Asian countries are discussed only in a general "global manufacturing" context, and it cannot be concluded that the three firms studied actually reshored production from Asia specifically. This directly corroborates the earlier note's flag that the WebSearch-summary sentence claiming an explicit "Asia and China" destination was unreliable — the docx reaches the same conclusion from the source-level analysis, strengthening (not contradicting) that caution.

**Important correction on evidence strength (docx self-corrects a NotebookLM overstatement):** the docx notes NotebookLM's classification of A14 as "direct empirical evidence for implementation barriers" is too strong. The DEMATEL analysis empirically measures three experts' assessments of criteria relationships, but barriers such as hollowed-out competencies, plant/equipment investment, management-time disruption, and quality/consistency problems during implementation may be drawn from the literature review/theoretical discussion rather than being separately tested DEMATEL variables. Unless confirmed as distinct DEMATEL variables actually scored by the three experts, these should be coded as supporting/literature-based, not direct case findings.

**Barriers identified (per docx), with status:**
1. **SUPPLY_PROCESS_COMPLEXITY** (Cost/Operational + Cultural/Organizational; Decision/Planning stage) — the strongest empirical result of the study; status **Direct DEMATEL result**. Reshoring must be treated as a restructuring of interconnected processes, not merely a change of geographic location.
2. **HIGH_RESHORING_CAPITAL_INVESTMENT** (Cost/Operational; Planning/Implementation) — plant, machinery, equipment, installation, technology, ramp-up; status **supporting implementation evidence**. Docx notes this combines well with A13's concrete UK case on machinery/tooling/software needs.
3. **DOMESTIC_PRODUCT_PROCESS_COMPETENCY_LOSS** (Asset/Capability + Human Resources/Knowledge; Reintegration) — status **supporting literature-based evidence**, reinforcing the domestic-capability-erosion cluster (aligns with A04, A06, A09, A11, A12, A13).
4. **MANAGEMENT_ATTENTION_BURDEN** (Cultural/Organizational + Cost/Operational; Implementation) — significant management involvement in coordination, supplier replacement, production setup, troubleshooting, training, quality stabilization; status **supporting evidence**.
5. **TRANSITION_QUALITY_INSTABILITY** (Cost/Operational; Ramp-up) — temporary quality/consistency deterioration during the launch of new domestic production (distinct from poor offshore-supplier quality); status **supporting evidence**.

**Items explicitly excluded from the Barrier Matrix per docx (drivers/decision criteria, not implementation barriers):** environmental sustainability, social responsibility, COVID-19, desire to restore control, supply-chain speed, operational flexibility, innovation, general cost efficiency.

**Terminology caution flagged in the docx:** the paper's own reshoring definition reportedly includes both return to the home country *and* relocation to another country in the same region — broader than the thesis's approved definition (reshoring = return to home country only; nearshoring/re-offshoring/right-shoring are distinct). The docx explicitly warns not to import A14's terminology into the thesis's analytical framework without this caveat.

**Citation-format correction (MINOR but concrete, worth carrying into the thesis's reference list/APA check):** the docx flags that "4873" is the article number, not a page number — a citation such as "(Pourhejazy & Ashby, 2021, p. 4873)" would be incorrect APA usage. For direct quotes, the actual PDF page (e.g., "PDF p. 6") or section/paragraph should be used instead, since the journal does not use traditional stable pagination.

**Theoretical framing (per docx):** TCE fits supply-process complexity, transition costs, monitoring/coordination, incomplete cost assessment, and supplier/contractual problems. RBV fits lost product/process competencies, technical knowledge, and domestic capabilities/plant/equipment resources. Triple Bottom Line could apply to the sustainability/social-responsibility criteria but is secondary to TCE/RBV for the thesis's framework.

**Overall docx verdict:** A14 remains **supporting empirical decision-analysis literature** (supply-process complexity, capital requirements, competency restoration, sustainability/management burden) — not Asia-specific core evidence and not to be used as direct proof of barriers to reshoring from China. This matches and reinforces the corpus classification and the existing note; no contradiction found, only added specificity and one explicit self-correction of an evidence-strength overstatement.
