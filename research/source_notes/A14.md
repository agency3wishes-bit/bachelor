## What could be established from available search snippets
High confidence on study design and headline finding; the corpus characterization is well corroborated.

The abstract (retrieved via WebSearch summary drawing on the MDPI/PMC listing) states the study uses an **extended fuzzy DEMATEL (Decision-Making Trial and Evaluation Laboratory)** method to analyze interrelationships among reshoring decision criteria and their sequential effects, using **data from the UK apparel industry as a baseline**. The stated motivation is that "myopic financial factors that once made overseas locations attractive for offshoring" are being revised, and COVID-19 "highlighted the need for restoring the previously offshored competencies." The method is explicitly framed as a **decision-support/decision-analysis tool** (expert-based, DEMATEL), not an implementation-process case study.

Headline finding: **supply process complexity** has the highest potential impact on the financial criterion among the decision criteria studied; **environmental sustainability** also strongly influences cost-efficiency, which the abstract frames as the major driver of the original offshoring decisions.

One search summary sentence asserted that "the study examined apparel reshoring decisions **to Asia and China** by analyzing how various decision criteria interrelate..." — **this specific claim (explicit China/Asia destination framing) is flagged as UNVERIFIED.** It appeared only in the WebSearch tool's own generated summary text, not inside a quoted abstract passage, and it is not consistent with the directly quoted abstract text (which describes the UK apparel industry as the data baseline for a general reshoring-decision-criteria study, without naming Asia or China as the offshoring origin). This should not be relied upon or cited; it may be a search-summary overreach rather than a genuine finding of the paper. Apparel-industry offshoring commonly involves Asian manufacturing origins as background context, but the paper's own Asia-specificity, per the corpus, is only "Low–Medium," which is consistent with treating this as background/plausible rather than confirmed.
