## What could be established from available search snippets
Abstract-level information only; full text was not accessible (ScienceDirect blocked). Search snippets gave a reasonably detailed and consistent summary of the paper's design and headline finding.

- The study addresses a stated gap: while prior research shows a firm's home country shapes internationalization decisions generally (investment choices, location selection), little was known about the relationship between home country and **reshoring processes specifically**.
- Method: a quantitative analysis of a **secondary dataset of 529 cross-industry reshoring projects**, developed by companies headquartered in **five countries: the United States, Germany, the United Kingdom, France, and Italy**.
- Headline finding: reshoring projects differ across these home countries in terms of **industry, entry mode, firm size, and motivations**. The paper's overall conclusion, per a search-result paraphrase, is that reshoring "is a phenomenon where each country has its own peculiarities" — i.e., home-country context substantially shapes how and why reshoring occurs.
- No search snippet specified which host (offshore/Asian) countries the reshoring projects originated from, nor did any snippet mention China or other Asian markets by name in connection with this dataset. The five countries identified in the search results are all **home** countries (reshoring destinations), not host countries being reshored from.
