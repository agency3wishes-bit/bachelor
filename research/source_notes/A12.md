## What could be established from available search snippets
High confidence on the study's design and headline findings; the corpus's characterization is fully consistent with what search snippets show.

Multiple independent sources (Springer, SDU research portal, RePEc/IDEAS, ResearchGate) converge on the same description: a **quantitative questionnaire survey of 152 offshoring manufacturing firms**, fielded in **2022**, examining how reshoring and reshoring intentions relate to (a) investment in Industry 4.0 / automation technologies, (b) COVID-19-related supply chain disruptions, and (c) "made-in" effects (i.e., country-of-origin / employee attachment to domestic production). The firms surveyed appear to be **Danish manufacturing firms** (consistent with the SDU-affiliated author team and journal indexing), though this national scope was inferred from author affiliation and portal context rather than from an explicit quoted sentence in the snippets — flag as **reasonably likely but not a directly quoted confirmation**.

Reported findings:
- Reshoring and reshoring intentions are **positively associated with investment in manufacturing automation**.
- Reshoring and reshoring intentions are **positively associated with "employee made-in"** (interpreted as an employee-level attachment/identification effect tied to domestic production, per the "made-in effects" framing in the title).
- COVID-19-related disruptions increased reshoring intentions **similarly across firm sizes**, but reactions to other, more familiar disruption types diverged by size: **larger firms decreased reshoring intentions as uncertainty increased**, while **smaller firms increased reshoring intentions as uncertainty increased**.

No search snippet gave any Asia-specific or host-country-disaggregated finding — consistent with the corpus note that "host countries are aggregated."

**Claim 1:** Investment in automation/Industry 4.0 technologies is positively associated with reshoring and reshoring intentions.
- Driver vs. implementation barrier: **Driver/enabler of the decision**, not an implementation barrier — this is an association with the decision/intention variable, not an observed obstacle during implementation.
- Firm-level vs. macro-level: Firm-level (survey of 152 firms), but the relationship is a decision/intention-stage correlation, not an implementation-process observation.
- Asia-specific: **No** — host countries aggregated, not disaggregated by Asian origin.
- Suggested framework category: **Does not map cleanly** to an implementation barrier category; could inform Discussion-level commentary on technology as a decision driver (Asset/Capability relevance only if reframed as a capability precondition, but the source itself measures the decision stage, not the implementation stage).
- Verification status: **PARTIAL** — the association itself is corroborated across multiple snippets, but no page-level detail (effect size, model specification) could be verified.

**Claim 2:** COVID-19 disruptions raised reshoring intentions across firm sizes; reactions to general uncertainty/disruption diverge by firm size (larger firms less reshoring-inclined under uncertainty, smaller firms more so).
- Driver vs. implementation barrier: **Driver** (affects the intention/decision to reshore), not an implementation barrier.
- Firm-level vs. macro-level: Firm-level, moderated by firm size.
- Asia-specific: **No.**
- Suggested framework category: Not applicable to the four implementation-barrier categories; relevant only as decision-stage/driver context in Introduction or Literature Review.
- Verification status: **PARTIAL** — corroborated across snippets but not checked against full text for exact statistical detail.
