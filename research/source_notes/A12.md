## What could be established from available search snippets
High confidence on the study's design and headline findings; the corpus's characterization is fully consistent with what search snippets show.

Multiple independent sources (Springer, SDU research portal, RePEc/IDEAS, ResearchGate) converge on the same description: a **quantitative questionnaire survey of 152 offshoring manufacturing firms**, fielded in **2022**, examining how reshoring and reshoring intentions relate to (a) investment in Industry 4.0 / automation technologies, (b) COVID-19-related supply chain disruptions, and (c) "made-in" effects (i.e., country-of-origin / employee attachment to domestic production). The firms surveyed appear to be **Danish manufacturing firms** (consistent with the SDU-affiliated author team and journal indexing), though this national scope was inferred from author affiliation and portal context rather than from an explicit quoted sentence in the snippets — flag as **reasonably likely but not a directly quoted confirmation**.

Reported findings:
- Reshoring and reshoring intentions are **positively associated with investment in manufacturing automation**.
- Reshoring and reshoring intentions are **positively associated with "employee made-in"** (interpreted as an employee-level attachment/identification effect tied to domestic production, per the "made-in effects" framing in the title).
- COVID-19-related disruptions increased reshoring intentions **similarly across firm sizes**, but reactions to other, more familiar disruption types diverged by size: **larger firms decreased reshoring intentions as uncertainty increased**, while **smaller firms increased reshoring intentions as uncertainty increased**.

No search snippet gave any Asia-specific or host-country-disaggregated finding — consistent with the corpus note that "host countries are aggregated."

## Barrier-relevant claims (coded)

**Claim 1:** Investment in automation/Industry 4.0 technologies is positively associated with reshoring and reshoring intentions.
- Driver vs. implementation barrier: **Driver/enabler of the decision**, not an implementation barrier — this is an association with the decision/intention variable, not an observed obstacle during implementation.
- Firm-level vs. macro-level: Firm-level (survey of 152 firms), but the relationship is a decision/intention-stage correlation, not an implementation-process observation.
- Asia-specific: **No** — host countries aggregated, not disaggregated by Asian origin.
- Suggested framework category: **Does not map cleanly** to an implementation barrier category; could inform Discussion-level commentary on technology as a decision driver (Asset/Capability relevance only if reframed as a capability precondition, but the source itself measures the decision stage, not the implementation stage).
- Verification status: **PARTIAL** — the association itself is corroborated across multiple snippets, but no page-level detail (effect size, model specification) could be verified.

**Claim 2:** COVID-19 disruptions raised reshoring intentions across firm sizes; reactions to general uncertainty/disruption diverge by firm size (larger firms less reshoring-inclined under uncertainty, smaller firms more so).
- Driver vs. implementation barrier: **Driver** (affects the intention/decision to reshore), not an implementation barrier.
- Firm-level vs. macro-level: Firm-level, moderated by firm size.
- Asia-specific: **No.**
- Suggested framework category: Not applicable to the four implementation-barrier categories; relevant only as decision-stage/driver context in Introduction or Literature Review.
- Verification status: **PARTIAL** — corroborated across snippets but not checked against full text for exact statistical detail.

## Supplementary evidence from source_coding_notes.docx

Content below is drawn from a prior working conversation (resources/source_coding_notes.docx) that appears to have analyzed the source via NotebookLM against the actual PDF; treat as higher-confidence than the WebSearch-only evidence above, but still cite verification status per claim.

**Design/sample (DIRECT per docx):** Quantitative questionnaire study of **152 Danish manufacturing companies with offshoring experience**. This confirms — at a higher confidence level than the earlier "reasonably likely but not directly quoted" inference — that the sample is Danish. Focus: actual reshoring and reshoring intentions.

**Confirms corpus classification, with an explicit self-correction in the docx.** The docx author notes that NotebookLM's own first-pass classification overstated A12 as "direct empirical evidence for implementation barriers," and corrects this: the survey/regression directly tests reshoring/reshoring-intentions against automation investment, Industry 4.0, supply-chain disruptions, made-in effects, and firm size — **not** against competence gaps, supply-network readiness, offshore specialization, or SME resource constraints. Those latter items may come from the paper's theoretical background/discussion/prior literature rather than being separately measured survey variables, and should not be treated as direct quantitative findings unless confirmed.

**Host-country/Asia specificity — CONFIRMS the existing note's caution, with more explicit detail.** The docx states plainly that offshore locations in the paper are described in general terms ("abroad," "low-wage countries," "foreign manufacturing locations"), not disaggregated by country. China appears only in general context — geopolitical tensions, friendshoring, international disruptions — with **no indication that a meaningful share of the 152 companies reshored specifically from China**, and no indication that the regression results pertain to China-to-Denmark reshoring. Docx explicitly rates: Asia relevance 2.5/10; Direct Asia implementation evidence 1/10. This strengthens (does not contradict) the existing note's "No Asia-specific finding" conclusion.

**Direct empirical findings (per docx, DIRECT):**
- Automation/Industry 4.0 investment is associated with reshoring intentions.
- COVID-19-related disruptions are associated with reshoring intentions.
- Employee "made-in" preferences are associated with intentions.
- The effect of disruptions differs by firm size.
- Current Industry 4.0 usage is not necessarily associated with *actual* reshoring (a nuance not present in the earlier WebSearch-based note — worth adding: intention/usage effects and actual-reshoring effects are reported as distinct, not identical).

**Supporting/unconfirmed implementation observations (per docx, explicitly labeled non-direct):** offshore-specialization dependence (code OFFSHORE_SPECIALIZATION_DEPENDENCE, Asset/Capability, status: conceptual/literature-supported, not yet a direct survey finding); domestic competency readiness gap (DOMESTIC_COMPETENCY_READINESS_GAP, Asset/Capability + Human Resources, status: supporting observation); domestic supply-network readiness (DOMESTIC_SUPPLY_NETWORK_READINESS, Cost/Operational + Asset/Capability, status: supporting observation — docx notes this overlaps with A11's hollowed-out domestic supply base theme); SME financial/human-resource constraints (SME_RESOURCE_CONSTRAINTS, Asset/Capability + Cost/Operational, status: contextual/theoretical unless directly measured); Industry 4.0 knowledge gap (INDUSTRY_4_0_KNOWLEDGE_GAP, Asset/Capability + Technological, status: supporting evidence). None of these should be counted as direct empirical barrier confirmations from A12 — the docx is explicit that this would require confirming they were actually included as survey instrument variables, which is not established.

**Theoretical framing (docx suggestion, not confirmed as the paper's own explicit framework):** Dynamic Capabilities is proposed as the best fit for explaining the intention-to-implementation gap (redesigning supply chains, reconfiguring resources, acquiring technological capability); RBV is suggested for specialized offshore capabilities/domestic skills/financial and technological resources; TCE is suggested for control costs, coordination difficulties, asset specificity, switching costs. The docx flags that it is **not verified whether the article itself explicitly uses TCE/RBV** or whether this is the docx author's own interpretive overlay — do not present these as the paper's declared theoretical lenses without further check.

**Best use in the thesis (per docx):** Introduction/Literature Review/Discussion — particularly to illustrate the gap between reshoring *intention* and actual *implementation* — rather than as Results-chapter direct barrier evidence. This matches the corpus's "Use in barrier synthesis: No."

**No contradiction with the existing WebSearch-based note** — the docx corroborates and sharpens it (confirms Danish sample, confirms low Asia relevance with an explicit rationale, and adds the useful intention-vs-actual-reshoring distinction and the caution that several "implementation" items may not be direct survey findings).
