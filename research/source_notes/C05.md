## Explicit boundary check

This source does **not** describe genuine reshoring in the thesis's strict sense (an Asian-host-to-home-country return of manufacturing observed at firm level). It is a **macro-level policy brief on geoeconomic fragmentation and supply-chain restructuring**, addressing phenomena such as global value chain lengthening/rerouting and continued economic dependence on China at the level of trade flows and country aggregates — not firm-level implementation. Three points bear directly on this boundary:

1. **Not firm-level, not peer-reviewed.** Per the corpus's own classification, this is a central-bank policy brief, not a peer-reviewed academic study and not an observation of any individual firm's reshoring implementation process. It carries no L1/L2 implementation evidentiary weight and must not be cited as if it were empirical firm-level support.
2. **Describes fragmentation/rerouting, not reshoring.** What could be established from search snippets (value chains lengthening, involving Chinese suppliers and US customers) describes a **rerouting or diversification pattern within global value chains** — potentially closer to nearshoring, "friend-shoring," or supply-chain diversification away from direct bilateral dependence — rather than a return of production to the home country. If anything, "lengthening" of value chains involving China is the opposite direction from reshoring (a shortening/domestic-return pattern), reinforcing that this source describes a distinct macro trend, not reshoring itself.
3. **High "Asia specificity" is contextual, not evidentiary for barriers.** The corpus labels this source "High context" for Asia specificity because China dependence is a central macro theme, but this is background/context relevance for framing the Introduction and Discussion, not evidence usable in the Results/barrier-synthesis chapters.
