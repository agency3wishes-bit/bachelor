## What the full text establishes (all DIRECT — full text read)
- Nature: a guest **editorial**, not an empirical study or systematic review. Six authors including three of the A02 team (Boffelli, Fratocchi, Kalchschmidt) — never count alongside A02 as independent evidence.
- Central claim: COVID-19 "can be associated with the role of a **trigger** for reshoring decisions … expect it to foster and accelerate decisions that have not been made yet" (p. 132 of the PDF pagination). Supported by short-term press-sourced examples (French thermometer producer returning production after Chinese supplier closure; Italian PPE producer restarting domestic production) and long-term expectations (risk-reduction relocations, supply-chain-level and policy-driven initiatives).
- Framework: a 2×2 of time frame (short/long term) × decision-maker (single firm / supply chain) for COVID-triggered reshoring decisions (their Fig. 1).
- Definition caution: the editorial defines reshoring as relocation "either back to the home country (back-reshoring) **or to a nearby country** … (near-shoring)" — broader than the thesis definition; any citation must not import this broader definition.
- Policy content: post-COVID policy measures (Japan financing relocation costs; Singapore, Australia, India programmes) — usable as Introduction/Discussion policy context.
- Implementation content: none of its own; implementation is touched only via citations to other work (Benstead et al. framework; Boffelli & Johansson meta-synthesis; preparation stage via Nujen et al.).
