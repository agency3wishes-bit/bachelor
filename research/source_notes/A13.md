**Skills/machinery/training/ramp-up barriers — NOW CONFIRMED (resolves the "UNVERIFIED" flag in the note above).** The docx lists five barriers explicitly coded as "Direct empirical" in its own Barrier Matrix table:
1. **DOMESTIC_COMPETENCY_GAP** (Asset/Capability + Human Resources; stage Preparation→Reintegration) — the firm initially lacked the knowledge needed to produce the part independently.
2. **MACHINERY_AVAILABILITY_GAP** (Asset/Capability + Cost/Operational; stage Preparation→Implementation) — the firm had to identify needed technology, locate suitable equipment, acquire machines, and integrate them into production; framed as rebuilding a production system, not merely a high-cost purchase.
3. **WORKFORCE_TRAINING_LEARNING_CURVE** (Asset/Capability + Cultural/Organizational; stage Implementation→Ramp-up) — described as the strongest part of the case: the company initially could not operate the new equipment; process involved mastering machines, training a specific employee, accumulating practical experience, eliminating errors, and reaching acceptable product quality over ~12 months.
4. **COMPLEMENTARY_TOOLING_SOFTWARE_GAP** (Cost/Operational + Asset/Capability–Technology; stage Implementation) — acquiring the main machine alone was insufficient; the firm additionally needed tools, tooling suppliers, software, technical support, and specialised services. Docx frames this as an important conceptual point: production capability is not a single machine but an interconnected system of equipment, knowledge, suppliers, and software.
5. **REGIONAL_ECOSYSTEM_READINESS_GAP** (Asset/Capability + Regulatory/Institutional; stage Planning→Reintegration) — implementation depended on regional availability of skilled labour, specialised suppliers, equipment providers, policy support, chambers of commerce, and universities/training institutions; framed as manifesting more as added search/development time than absolute resource absence.
