## GLOBAL DRAFTING RULES (apply to every paragraph of Chapter 4)

1. **Results reports; it does not interpret.** No TCE/RBV language, no managerial implications, no "this suggests firms should…". Any interpretive pull is flagged inline as `→ belongs in Ch5`.
2. **Evidence types are never summed.** Forbidden: "supported by nine sources." Permitted: "reported as a direct case finding in two studies, in three literature reviews, and argued conceptually in two further sources." Always decompose by type.
3. **No page numbers** anywhere (no verified pagination exists for any source).
4. **No unverified specifics:** no A10 sample-size figure (105 vs. 33-from-96 unresolved); no C03 country percentages; no A11 "11.64%" TCA figure; no A18 firm identity/name; no A10 sample sizes; A15 must **not** be cited as a source for theme 1.1 (skills) or 1.2 (machinery) — its versions are UNVERIFIED/provisional and may be sector-general.
5. **Asia-specific framing** is permitted only for **A13** (China→UK) and **A18** (China→Italy) as direct single-case evidence, and for **A02/A04** only with explicit partial-attribution caveats. **A09 findings must be presented as general Nordic/Swedish evidence** — never as China-specific.
6. **A03 is never cited for a barrier claim** (framework/terminology only; contributes nothing to the matrix).
7. **British English** throughout, except the verbatim research questions.
8. **Mandatory wording:** A18 supplier reaction is an **"adverse response"**, never "hostile"; A13 findings are framed as **"a detailed UK case demonstrates…"**, never generalised; A10 workforce-resistance is phrased only as **"potential employee resistance during closure of foreign facilities; the evidence is limited and context-dependent"** — never "Chinese workers may sabotage".
9. **Review evidence** (A01, A06, A07, A10, A11, A17) is reported as review-level and **must not be counted as primary confirmation**; A06 should not stand alone as the sole evidence for a barrier — pair it with a direct source. **Conceptual evidence** (A16, A25) is reported as theoretically argued, never as empirical frequency.

# 4.5 Asset/Capability Barriers (~600 w)

**Section purpose:** Report the most broadly supported category: the domestic skills/competence gap, the capacity/machinery/tooling gap, host-supplier knowledge lock-in, GVC stickiness, and regional ecosystem readiness — with A13 and A18 as the two Asia-specific anchor cases, framed as single cases.

**Ordering logic:** lead with theme 1.1 (the single most broadly supported barrier, across all three evidence types), then 1.2 (capacity/machinery), then 1.3 (host-supplier lock-in, A18 anchor), then 1.4 (GVC stickiness, review+conceptual only), then 1.5 (regional ecosystem, A13 single case). Direct empirical before review before conceptual within each theme; state the breadth of support by type without summing.

### Paragraph 4.5-P1 — Theme 1.1: Domestic skilled-labour & competence gap
- **Topic-sentence claim:** The loss of domestic skilled labour and manufacturing competence — with the associated training burden and learning curve — is the most broadly supported barrier in the corpus, appearing in direct case evidence, across the review literature, and in conceptual argument.
- **Evidence items (direct before review before conceptual; report breadth by type, never summed):**
  - **A13** (Pegoraro et al., 2022) — direct empirical (L1), single case. Verification: PARTIAL-to-DIRECT (docx confirms via classification table). Asia-specific: **Yes** (China→UK). Claim wording supported: "in a China-to-UK case, the firm initially lacked the knowledge to produce the part independently; workforce training and a learning curve — mastering new equipment, training a specific employee, accumulating practical experience and reaching acceptable quality — extended over roughly a twelve-month period."
  - **A09** (Li et al., 2025) — direct empirical. Verification: PARTIAL. Asia-specific: No (general Nordic/Swedish). Claim wording supported: "an internal competence gap — competencies lost over years of offshoring — was identified among the Swedish firms."
  - **A04** (Di Mauro et al., 2018) — direct empirical (limited). Verification: PARTIAL. Asia-specific: Partial. Claim wording supported: "a scarcity of specialised manpower and loss of domestic competences were reported as post-decision constraints on implementing the return." (Quote is truncated in the corpus — do not reconstruct; no page.)
  - **A20** (Mirzaei et al., 2021) — direct empirical. Verification: PARTIAL. Asia-specific: No. Claim wording supported: "a domestic skilled-labour shortage — loss of tacit knowledge, deficit of core manufacturing competencies, and high training time/cost — was identified."
  - **A06** — review. Claim wording supported: "skilled-labour shortage and an internal capability gap are reported as barriers in the review."
  - **A11** — review. Claim wording supported: "a technical-skills shortage and a generational manufacturing-skills gap are identified as implementation concerns in the reshoring literature."
  - **A17** — review. Claim wording supported: "a digital/technical-skills shortage is reported."
  - **A16** — conceptual. Claim wording supported: "a domestic skilled-workforce shortage is conceptually argued."
  - **A25** — conceptual. Claim wording supported: "a domestic labour-availability gap is conceptually identified."
- **Mandatory caveats (hard):** **A15 is NOT cited here** — its skills version is UNVERIFIED/possibly sector-general (rules 4). A13 framed as a single detailed UK case, not generalised (rule 8); the ~12-month figure is one case's ramp-up, reported as such, not a norm. A09/A04/A20 are general/partial, not China-specific (rule 5). Report the breadth explicitly by type ("direct case evidence in … ; review evidence in … ; conceptual argument in …"), never as a single count (rule 2).

### Paragraph 4.5-P2 — Theme 1.2: Domestic capacity, facilities & machinery gap (incl. complementary tooling/software)
- **Topic-sentence claim:** Rebuilding production capacity at home is reported as more than acquiring a machine: it requires facilities, equipment, and complementary tooling, software and technical services — an interconnected production system.
- **Evidence items (direct before review before conceptual):**
  - **A13** — direct empirical (L1), single case. Verification: PARTIAL-to-DIRECT. Asia-specific: **Yes** (China→UK). Claim wording supported: "the firm had to identify the needed technology, locate and acquire suitable machines and integrate them, and additionally obtain tooling, tooling suppliers, software, technical support and specialised services — framed as rebuilding a production system rather than a single high-cost purchase (machinery-availability gap; complementary tooling/software gap)."
  - **A09** — direct empirical. Verification: PARTIAL. Asia-specific: No. Claim wording supported: "a domestic capacity shortage and facilities/equipment constraint were identified despite a reshoring decision having been made."
  - **A20** — direct empirical. Verification: PARTIAL. Asia-specific: No. Claim wording supported: "a domestic production-capacity gap — insufficient facilities, equipment limits, production-volume limits and insufficient investment resources — was identified."
  - **A06** — review. Claim wording supported: "a domestic-capacity shortage and infrastructure lock-out (loss of facilities and know-how after prolonged offshoring) are reported in the review."
  - **A16** — conceptual. Claim wording supported: "domestic capacity rebuilding is embedded in the conceptual account of new fixed costs."
  - **A25** — conceptual. Claim wording supported: "a domestic infrastructure/resource gap is conceptually noted (the term 'infrastructure' is ambiguous in the source)."
- **Mandatory caveats:** **A15 not counted** (rule 4). A13 single case. A13's "production capability is a system, not a machine" framing is the strongest articulation — report as the case's characterisation.

### Paragraph 4.5-P3 — Theme 1.3: Host-supplier dependence & knowledge lock-in
- **Topic-sentence claim:** Specialised capabilities concentrated with the host-country supplier can be difficult to repatriate, limiting how much production can actually return — shown most concretely in a China-to-Italy case.
- **Evidence items (direct before review before conceptual):**
  - **A18** (Pedroletti, 2025) — direct empirical (L1), single case. Verification: abstract-level DIRECT for "restrain access to essential resources and capabilities"; detail PARTIAL. Asia-specific: **Yes** (Chinese suppliers; China→Italy). Claim wording supported: "persistent dependence on specialised Chinese supplier capabilities constrained full reshoring; the case lacked the necessary production knowledge, experience with the components, specialised sourcing skills and suitable domestic suppliers, so partial reshoring functioned as a response to the practical impossibility of fully replacing the Chinese network (full-reshoring feasibility constraint)."
  - **A09** — direct empirical. Verification: PARTIAL. Asia-specific: No. Claim wording supported: "supplier/IP dependence — the firm may not fully control rights to a component or technology and therefore cannot simply move production home — was identified."
  - **A06** — review. Claim wording supported: "host-supplier dependence (risk of losing supplier knowledge/components) is reported in the review."
  - **A17** — review. Claim wording supported: "offshore knowledge dependence is reported (source-context check noted in the matrix)."
  - **A25** — conceptual. Claim wording supported: "reshoring knowledge-transfer ambiguity — knowledge residing with offshore employees, a foreign subsidiary, an external supplier or the host-country network — is conceptually identified."
- **Mandatory caveats (hard):** A18's domestic know-how gap is **narrow/specialised**, not a claim that Italy lacked manufacturing capacity in general — do not overstate. Single detailed case (rule 8). No firm identity, no page numbers (rule 4). `→` linking this to RBV/TCE (asset specificity, resource rebuilding) belongs in Ch5.

### Paragraph 4.5-P4 — Theme 1.4: GVC stickiness / offshore sunk-cost inertia
- **Topic-sentence claim:** Established global value chains are reported as "sticky": accumulated offshore sunk investment and supplier relationships create inertia that impedes unwinding offshored activity — a mechanism argued conceptually and echoed in the review literature, with no direct case evidence.
- **Evidence items (review before conceptual; no direct empirical exists):**
  - **A17** — review. Verification: PARTIAL. Asia-specific: No. Claim wording supported: "offshore sunk-cost inertia is reported — sunk costs create inertia against the decision to leave the offshore location."
  - **A16** — conceptual (core argument). Verification: PARTIAL. Asia-specific: No. Claim wording supported: "value chains are conceptually argued to be sticky, particularly for large firms, owing to sunk investment, accumulated supplier relationships, specialised infrastructure, location-specific knowledge and GVC interdependence."
- **Mandatory caveats (hard):** Keep A16's **offshore sunk cost** (already incurred; explains why firms do not leave) strictly distinct from its **new fixed costs of the return** (reported under theme 2.2). State plainly that no direct empirical evidence supports this theme — it is review and conceptual only (rules 2, 9).

### Paragraph 4.5-P5 — Theme 1.5: Regional ecosystem readiness
- **Topic-sentence claim:** A detailed UK case demonstrates that reshoring implementation depended on the readiness of the home region — skilled labour, specialised suppliers, equipment providers and supporting institutions — with gaps manifesting as added search and development time rather than absolute absence.
- **Evidence items (direct before review):**
  - **A13** (Pegoraro et al., 2022) — direct empirical (L1), single case. Verification: PARTIAL-to-DIRECT. Asia-specific: **Yes** via A13 (China→UK). Claim wording supported: "implementation depended on regional availability of skilled labour, specialised suppliers, equipment providers, policy support and training institutions; where these were thin, the gap manifested as added search and development time (regional ecosystem-readiness gap)."
  - **A17** — review. Verification: PARTIAL. Asia-specific: No. Claim wording supported: "a digital-ecosystem/partnership gap is reported — digital servitisation requires interaction with technology providers, data specialists, suppliers and research institutions, whose absence becomes a barrier."
- **Mandatory caveats (hard):** Frame strictly as **"a detailed UK case demonstrates…"**; do not generalise the single case (rule 8). The regional ecosystem concerns the **home** region, not the Asian host, so this theme is Asia-specific only in the sense that the case is a China→UK return. `→` policy/managerial recommendations on ecosystem-building belong in Ch5.
