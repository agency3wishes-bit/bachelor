# Front Matter

## Abstract

**[STATUS: PROVISIONAL — drafted after Chapters 1–5; to be rechecked once Chapter 6 (Conclusion) is complete and the final PRISMA figures are recorded.]**

International manufacturing has been shaped by decades of offshoring to Asian host countries, yet changing risk conditions have renewed interest in returning production home. While the reasons why firms consider reshoring are well documented, far less is known about the difficulties they face when they attempt to carry it out. This thesis asks what central barriers manufacturing firms face when implementing reshoring strategies from Asian markets. Using a systematic literature review of an approved corpus of peer-reviewed reshoring research, with a formal analytical sample of twenty-one studies, the thesis identifies eighteen barrier themes and organises them into a firm-level framework of four categories: regulatory/institutional, cultural/organisational, cost/operational, and asset/capability barriers. The synthesis indicates that the most broadly supported barriers concern the reconstruction of domestic capability — skills, machinery, and supplier ecosystems — together with dependence on specialised host-country suppliers, underestimated operational adjustments, and host-country exit constraints. Interpreted through Transaction Cost Economics and the Resource-Based View, these barriers reflect the costs of dismantling committed offshore arrangements and the difficulty of rebuilding eroded resources at home. The thesis derives stage-based managerial implications, from capability audits and exit due diligence to phased returns and workforce development.
